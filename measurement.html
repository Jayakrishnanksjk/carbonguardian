Overview

Measurement, Monitoring, and Verification (MMV) is a critical framework used in the geological sequestration of CO₂. As the need to mitigate climate change intensifies, CO₂ storage technologies have emerged as vital solutions for reducing greenhouse gas emissions. MMV plays an essential role in ensuring the effectiveness, safety, and integrity of these storage projects.

MMV encompasses a range of activities designed to measure the amount of CO₂ that is injected, monitor its behavior within geological formations, and verify that it remains securely stored over time. This process not only helps in assessing the performance of CO₂ storage sites but also builds public trust and compliance with regulatory requirements.

Services

Application

Before CO₂ injection begins, a baseline assessment is conducted to establish initial conditions within the geological formation. This includes measuring existing CO₂ levels, pressure, temperature, and geological characteristics. These baseline measurements are crucial for comparing future data and assessing the impact of CO₂ injection.

During the injection phase, real-time monitoring technologies are employed to track the movement of CO₂. Techniques such as:

• Seismic Monitoring: Utilizing seismic surveys to visualize the subsurface and monitor CO₂ plume migration.
• Pressure and Temperature Sensors: Installing sensors to continuously measure changes in pressure and temperature, providing data on the behavior of the CO₂.

Once CO₂ is injected, verification processes are implemented to ensure that the injected CO₂ remains contained. This involves:

• Geochemical Analysis: Sampling groundwater and soil to detect any changes in chemical composition that may indicate leakage.
• Satellite and Aerial Surveys: Using remote sensing technology to identify any surface anomalies that could suggest CO₂ escape.

Long-term monitoring is essential to ensure the ongoing safety and effectiveness of CO₂ storage. This phase may last for decades and includes periodic assessments of:

• Structural Integrity: Evaluating the geological formation and cap rock to ensure they continue to contain the CO₂.
• Environmental Impact: Monitoring potential impacts on surrounding ecosystems and groundwater.

Regular reporting of MMV data to regulatory bodies is essential for compliance with environmental standards. This transparency helps in maintaining public confidence in CO₂ storage projects.
