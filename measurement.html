Overview

Measurement, Monitoring, and Verification (MMV) is a critical framework used in the geological sequestration of CO2. As the need to mitigate climate change intensifies, CO2 storage technologies have emerged as vital solutions for reducing greenhouse gas emissions. MMV plays an essential role in ensuring the effectiveness, safety, and integrity of these storage projects.

MMV encompasses a range of activities designed to measure the amount of CO2 that is injected, monitor its behavior within geological formations, and verify that it remains securely stored over time. This process not only helps in assessing the performance of CO2 storage sites but also builds public trust and compliance with regulatory requirements.

Services

Application

Before CO2 injection begins, a baseline assessment is conducted to establish initial conditions within the geological formation. This includes measuring existing CO2 levels, pressure, temperature, and geological characteristics. These baseline measurements are crucial for comparing future data and assessing the impact of CO2 injection.

During the injection phase, real-time monitoring technologies are employed to track the movement of CO2. Techniques such as:

• Seismic Monitoring: Utilizing seismic surveys to visualize the subsurface and monitor CO2 plume migration.
• Pressure and Temperature Sensors: Installing sensors to continuously measure changes in pressure and temperature, providing data on the behavior of the CO2.

Once CO2 is injected, verification processes are implemented to ensure that the injected CO2 remains contained. This involves:

• Geochemical Analysis: Sampling groundwater and soil to detect any changes in chemical composition that may indicate leakage.
• Satellite and Aerial Surveys: Using remote sensing technology to identify any surface anomalies that could suggest CO2 escape.

Long-term monitoring is essential to ensure the ongoing safety and effectiveness of CO2 storage. This phase may last for decades and includes periodic assessments of:

• Structural Integrity: Evaluating the geological formation and cap rock to ensure they continue to contain the CO2.
• Environmental Impact: Monitoring potential impacts on surrounding ecosystems and groundwater.

Regular reporting of MMV data to regulatory bodies is essential for compliance with environmental standards. This transparency helps in maintaining public confidence in CO2 storage projects.

At Carbon Guardian, we aim to drive the transition to a Net-Zero future by reducing industrial carbon footprints through strong technology and regulation—prioritizing climate action, safety, and societal well-being.
